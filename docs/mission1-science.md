# Mission 1: A Clue in the Cold

Audience: ages 7–12. Tone: curious, supportive, and safe to explore.

The outpost and its measurements are fictional. The two simulated scanner readings compare an ice-like light pattern in a shaded patch with a rock-like pattern at a nearby reference site. The report says **possible water ice**, and asks for further measurements. A reading in this game is not a new real-world discovery, and the sunlit reference does not imply the absence of water everywhere in sunlight.

## Science behind the observations

- Near the lunar poles, some crater interiors remain permanently shadowed and very cold. These conditions can preserve water ice. NASA overview: https://science.nasa.gov/moon/moon-water-and-ices/
- Scientists identified surface ice using characteristic light-absorption signatures measured by the Moon Mineralogy Mapper aboard Chandrayaan-1. NASA/JPL: https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/news/ice-confirmed-at-the-moons-poles/

The hand-held training scanner is an educational simplification of instrument-based observation and comparison. No physical temperature values, real geographic coordinates, or live instrument data are claimed.

The journal's Shackleton mosaic is explicitly labelled as a reference image, not a photograph of the fictional outpost. The latest completed entry is retained locally; replay replaces it only after another completed report. No names, accounts, or server are involved.

## Investigation sequence

Pip introduces an unexplained reading, then helps the player compare two samples.
At each site, align the scanner and collect a three-second simulated light reading.
Completed scans enter the journal. Canceling a scan leaves that observation available
for another attempt. Either site can be explored first.

At Communications, review both readings and confirm the cautious conclusion before
sending. The transmission ends with an acknowledgement from Earth and a journal
button. Dialogue reveals gradually, with Show full text available immediately.
Reduced-motion mode shows full dialogue and a static scan indicator.

The intended first visit takes roughly 3–5 minutes including exploration and reading.
Players control dialogue pacing. The scanner animation is an illustrative interface,
not a model of instrument optics. The player aligns a camera reticle in 3D or moves
the target in the 2D scanner preview using drag or arrow keys.
